# M1B held-out evaluation: seeds 3000–3999

M1B has two independently randomized enemy slots. All policies used the same 1,000 environment seeds. The heuristic heals at HP ≤ 10, attacks the living enemy with the lowest current HP (slot 0 breaks ties), uses Action Surge when no Action remains, then ends turn. PPO training used terminal-only reward, independent training seeds 0–2, 200,000 requested timesteps each (200,704 actual), shared hyperparameters, and model-selection seeds 1000–1199.

First-kill counts include fights that the Fighter later lost; the remaining fights had no kill. PPO win rates were **66.0%, 67.7%, 69.3%**; mean **67.67%**, sample standard deviation across training seeds **1.65 percentage points**. Training wall times were **35.49, 35.37, 35.18 seconds** for seeds 0–2.

The 1,000-episode gap between the heuristic and top PPO seed was 1.8 points, so a fresh non-overlapping 5,000-seed comparison used 4000–8999. It yielded Heuristic **3393/5000 (67.86%)** and PPO seeds 0–2 **3395/5000 (67.90%)**, **3421/5000 (68.42%)**, **3496/5000 (69.92%)**. Seed 2's 2.06-point lead over the heuristic persisted, but the other PPO seeds did not reproduce it; the three-seed mean is close to the heuristic. M1B is meaningfully harder than M1A, and its target decision exposes training-seed variation.

Traces show that PPO seed 0 preferred slot 1 much more than the other policies: it made 3681 slot-1 attacks versus 3273 slot-0 attacks, and its first kill was slot 1 in 568 fights versus slot 0 in 299. On seed 3003, the heuristic first attacked the lower-HP slot 1 while PPO seed 2 attacked slot 0 despite both targets being legal. This is a decision difference, not proof that the choice caused the final outcome because subsequent RNG draws may diverge.

# M2 terminal-reward held-out evaluation: seeds 3000–3999

M2 adds one-use simplified Cleave to the M1B two-enemy encounter. The heuristic heals at HP ≤ 10, uses Cleave while both enemies live, otherwise attacks the lowest-HP living target, uses Action Surge with no Action, then ends turn. All policies used the same 1,000 environment seeds. PPO trained with terminal-only reward for 200,000 requested timesteps per seed (200,704 actual), training seeds 0–2, shared hyperparameters, and model-selection seeds 1000–1199.

First-kill counts include fights later lost. PPO win rates were **66.6%, 64.8%, 66.3%**; mean **65.90%**, sample standard deviation across training seeds **0.96 percentage points**. Training wall times were **36.69, 38.48, 37.14 seconds** for seeds 0–2.

The best 1,000-episode PPO result exceeded the heuristic by 1.6 points, within evaluation uncertainty. A fresh non-overlapping 5,000-seed comparison (4000–8999) gave Heuristic **3316/5000 (66.32%)** and PPO seeds 0–2 **3388/5000 (67.76%)**, **3359/5000 (67.18%)**, **3414/5000 (68.28%)**. The gains are modest and policy differences remain substantial. M2 is meaningfully difficult, but the evidence does not show that Cleave itself improved wins: its heuristic win rate was below M1B's and two PPO seeds used it in under one quarter of fights.

The clearest trace difference is ability choice. In 537 of the heuristic-versus-PPO-seed-0 loss or truncation cases, the first divergence was the heuristic choosing Cleave while PPO chose a targeted attack. On seed 3000 both enemies were alive and Cleave legal; the heuristic used Cleave immediately while PPO attacked slot 1. PPO seed 0 used Cleave mostly after only one enemy remained, so its learned use resembles a limited extra attack more than the heuristic's multi-target rule. These first divergences describe behavior; they do not isolate causal effects after the dice streams diverge.

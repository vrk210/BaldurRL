Follow-up: the sampling defect was corrected and both stages rescored later on

Verdict: validate the main numerical results and the conclusion that these fixed
PPO checkpoints leave a measurable gap to the hand heuristics. Revise the causal
PPO diagnosis, qualify the meaning of decision headroom, and describe the original
evaluation range as reused after design feedback. I found no combat or simulator
reconstruction defect that explains the reported results.

Reproduction and validation

I reran all 16 policies in the two final v2 result tables: random, all four named
heuristics, and the three saved final PPO models, each on seeds 3000-7999.
All 80,000 encounter outcomes matched the original run cards seed by seed.
Every field of every summary also matched exactly, including rounds, remaining
HP, action counts, resource-use rates, first kills, wins, and truncations.

The original main win rates are therefore independently repeatable executions:

                        M5                         M6
naive                   61.44%                     40.92%
best heuristic          78.52% tuned                80.60% adaptive
PPO s0/s1/s2            74.90/74.68/73.56%          75.74/75.36/74.52%
PPO pooled              74.38%                     75.2067%
best minus naive        17.08 points               39.68 points
best minus PPO pooled    4.14 points                5.3933 points

The paired SEs and z statistics reproduced. In particular, the pooled calculation
averages the three model outcomes within each of 5,000 encounter seeds before
computing the paired SE; it does not incorrectly treat the three repetitions of
an encounter as independent. Its uncertainty is conditional on these three
fixed trained models, rather than a full estimate of variation from retraining.

All 312 repository tests passed; one test was skipped. I also generated 200
random-policy encounters on seeds 51000-51099, sampling states every fourth
decision, then checked every legal candidate action against an independently
copied live environment using the same newly supplied RNG seed. M5 matched on
3,681 transitions from 721 states; M6 matched on 3,604 transitions from 730 states.
Observations, rewards, termination/truncation, diagnostic info, and masks matched.
This tests restoration of the stochastic transition rules at ongoing decisions;
it does not claim to reconstruct the unobserved original live RNG stream.

An audit of the 80,000 saved main-table traces found no run-card outcome mismatch
and no recorded illegal action. The saved models' hyperparameters match the
reported defaults: 1,003,520 steps, four environments, 1,024 rollout steps,
batch size 256, ten epochs, gamma .99, entropy coefficient .01, learning rate
.0003, and separate [64,64] policy/value networks. Training metadata reports
411-453 seconds, consistent with the report's approximate seven minutes.

I checked archived paired and rollout-pilot calculations, and inspected the
checkpoint summaries and diagnostic source. I did not retrain PPO, rerun the
900-encounter rollout pilot, repeat its 1,024-rollout rescoring, or fully reproduce
the historical v1 policies and tuning sweeps. Those are narrower verification
limits than the exact, freshly executed final-v2 comparisons above.

Fresh-seed check

Without changing rules, thresholds, or checkpoints, I evaluated seeds
40000-44999, which were outside the report's documented training, selection,
development, and evaluation ranges. Each entry below has 5,000 encounters:

M5: naive 60.42%; trip_focus 75.38%; healer_first 72.58%; tuned 77.84%;
    PPO s0 74.76%.
M6: naive 41.52%; hold 79.88%; dive_all 78.10%; adaptive 81.02%;
    PPO s0 76.46%.

The broad ranking survives. Tuned beats naive by 17.42 points and PPO s0 by
3.08 points; adaptive beats naive by 39.50 points and PPO s0 by 4.56 points.
Fresh-seed adaptive minus hold is only 1.14 points, paired SE .598 points,
z=1.91 (approximate 95% interval -0.03 to +2.31). The small adaptive advantage
is a credible modest effect, but it is not decisively significant in this new
range. The original main gap remains strongly supported.

Finding 1: PPO's local scores do not establish its main causal mistakes.

Relevant report: lines 157-175; source: runs/v2/analysis/ppo_gap.py:17-32,34-51.
Priority: revise the explanation before using it to guide training changes.

The frequency counts span all three models, but the states scored do not form a
representative sample. The script keeps the first 12 states satisfying occurrence
count modulo 7 == 1, then stops sampling that kind. All six selected M6 kinds
come from PPO s0, within encounter seeds 3000-3191. Five M5 kinds are sampled
only from s0; the Cleave-to-Surge kind is sampled only from s1. For example,
M6 Cleave-to-Attack's 12 states occupy only 11 distinct fights in 3001-3032.
There is no reservoir sampling, stratification across models, or reported
uncertainty for the mean local advantage.

The values also use heuristic continuation, not PPO continuation. They answer
which first action is better if the heuristic takes over afterward. They do not
measure the effect of correcting that action while PPO continues playing. The
report explicitly warns that local values are not additive, but subsequently
multiplies their sample means by population frequencies and uses agreement with
the overall gap as an explanation. That explanatory step is not validated.

I tested the proposed three major M5 disagreement families over whole fights.
If tuned chose Trip and PPO chose Attack or Cleave, the Trip repair substituted
tuned's Trip. If tuned chose Cleave and PPO chose Attack, the Cleave repair
substituted Cleave. Both repairs combined these rules. Every other decision
remained deterministic masked PPO. Each configuration used all 5,000 original
encounter seeds for each of the three saved models:

                     s0        s1        s2        pooled
original PPO         74.90%    74.68%    73.56%    74.38%
Trip repair          75.12%    75.98%    74.86%    75.32%
Cleave repair        74.90%    73.76%    73.36%    74.0067%
both repairs         75.02%    76.84%    74.42%    75.4267%

Both repairs recover 1.0467 points of the 4.14-point pooled gap, leaving 3.0933
points to tuned. Seed-specific gains are .12 +/- .45, 2.16 +/- .74, and
.86 +/- .75 points (paired SE, not confidence bounds). Forcing Cleave alone
does not produce a consistent gain. The results support some Trip-timing value,
interactions, and model-specific effects; they do not substantiate a general
4-5-point causal explanation. This does not prove the proposed choices are
intrinsically wrong: PPO may fail to capitalize on a corrected action afterward.

For M6 I forced PPO s0 to follow the adaptive macro plan: immediately advance
when legal against an opening tanky Brute, and mask Advance against a soft Brute.
Other choices remained PPO. It won 76.18%, versus 75.74% originally, a .44-point
gain with paired SE .68; it remained 4.42 points behind adaptive (SE .64).
This supports an execution gap for this model under this intervention, but is
not a comprehensive decomposition of all three models' macro/sequence errors.

Recommended revision: present local rescoring as hypotheses under heuristic
continuation, use samples across models/encounters with uncertainty, and prefer
whole-fight interventions or values under PPO continuation for attribution.

Finding 2: the M6 headline spread mainly measures two basic baseline corrections.

Relevant report: lines 3,79,196-202; source: agents/tactical_heuristics.py:253-256.
Priority: qualify the decision-headroom claim, while retaining the measured gap.

Naive counts all living enemies before deciding to Cleave. At the line, while
the Brute lives, Cleave can reach only that Brute. Naive therefore spends its
two once-per-encounter Cleaves on one target for halved damage. This is a literal
transfer of the M4 policy into a stage with different reach, and is legal, but
it makes the baseline substantially weaker than a minimally adapted policy.

I changed only the target-count flag and/or the existing Trip flag, retaining
Second Wind threshold 10, Cleave threshold 2, lowest-HP targeting, and no Advance:

naive                                      40.92%
count reachable Cleave targets only         54.10%
use Trip only                              65.04%
use Trip and count reachable targets        77.70%
hold (additional threshold tuning)          78.88%
adaptive                                   80.60%

The two basic changes recover 36.78 of the 39.68 headline points, about 92.7%.
That leaves 2.90 points from the minimally adapted no-movement policy to adaptive.
The effects interact; the individual gains should not be added as causal shares.

The report already admits a new-button contribution on M5, where Trip alone
recovers 15.18 of 17.08 points. Extend that qualification to M6's reach mismatch.
The result shows much larger performance spread among the chosen policies and
a gap to this PPO setup. It does not measure an optimal value, the intrinsic
depth of decisions, or how much additional gain exists above the best heuristic.

Finding 3: seeds 3000-7999 were reused after test-informed design feedback.

Relevant report: lines 26-45,59,206. Priority: correct the experimental framing.

The report evaluates v1 on that range, diagnoses M5 as too easy from its 75.92%
naive win rate, then retunes the design and evaluates v2 on the same range.
Candidate comparisons and thresholds may indeed have used disjoint development
seeds, but the overall design process has already received evaluation feedback.
The range is therefore not an untouched test set for the whole design process.
This is not evidence of deliberate cherry-picking or a numerical error. The
fresh-seed results above substantially mitigate the concern. Describe the reused
benchmark honestly and use a new frozen range for future final claims.

Finding 4: the plateau and rollout root cause need weaker wording.

Relevant report: lines 131,179-190,203,210. Priority: distinguish observation
from extrapolation.

Four checkpoints from one training seed show no sustained gain between 500k
and 1M steps. They do not establish that longer training will not help, or that
PPO generally cannot close the gap. The demonstrated statement is about these
three default-hyperparameter 1M checkpoints and this limited learning curve.
Gamma .99 discounts by individual decision steps, whereas evaluation measures
undiscounted wins; an additional useful experiment would separate optimization
of that objective from combat execution. I did not test that hypothesis.

The archived rollout pilot really is worse than its continuation heuristic:
58.00% vs 78.83% on M5, and 65.33% vs 81.33% on M6, with the reported paired SEs.
Exact restoration plus noisy action estimates makes finite-sample selection
error a plausible explanation. However, early-state rescoring and one planner
seed at one budget do not quantify that it is mostly the cause. A budget sweep
with multiple planner seeds and matched encounters is needed for that stronger
claim. I did not run such a sweep, so this root-cause statement remains provisional.

Minor literal corrections

The report says every competent M5 policy spends all four Trip charges (line
153). Actual four-Trip fight rates are 90.44% for tuned and 89.84/89.36/92.24%
for PPO s0/s1/s2. Say approximately 3.9 Trips per fight or most fights use all four.

Same-family target-swap kinds in ppo_gap.py are split by the heuristic's target
slot. The table's generic 'Trip enemy X -> trip a different enemy' and 'attack
target swaps' rows are each one selected slot's kind, not an aggregate across
all slots. Label them specifically or aggregate all slots before reporting.

Completed sampling follow-up (2026-10-10)

The diagnostic now uses uniform reservoir sampling across the entire trace set,
separately within each model/disagreement stratum. It selects 12 states per
model per type, then weights means by the actual occurrence counts for those
models. Counts and taxonomy remain unchanged. There are 204 M5 and 216 M6 states;
M5 Cleave-to-Surge has no s0 occurrences, so that type has only 24 states.
All other types have 12 states from each model. The sample seed is 20261010.

Every selected state was freshly rescored using 1,024 common-seed rollouts per
candidate action with heuristic continuation: 860,160 simulated continuation
episodes in total. Approximate pointwise 95% normal intervals include state
sampling and paired Monte Carlo uncertainty, conditional on the fixed traces.
These are local action values, not a whole-fight attribution or an estimate of
variation from retraining PPO.

Selected old versus corrected local advantages (percentage points):

M5 Trip -> Attack:      +1.90 -> +1.53, interval [+0.82,+2.23]
M5 Cleave -> Attack:    +2.73 -> +0.12, interval [-1.49,+1.74]
M5 Trip -> Cleave:      +2.29 -> +2.70, interval [+1.82,+3.58]
M5 Trip slot 0 swap:    +1.00 -> -0.51, interval [-1.54,+0.52]
M6 Trip -> Attack:      +0.42 -> +0.94, interval [+0.37,+1.51]
M6 Advance -> Attack:   +0.66 -> -0.73, interval [-3.00,+1.53]
M6 Advance -> Trip:     +0.83 -> +0.15, interval [-0.44,+0.74]

The corrected M5 Cleave-to-Attack sample has model-specific means
+3.40/-0.09/+0.32 points for s0/s1/s2. The original sample covered only s0,
which accounts for 2.6% of that type's occurrences. Thus a claim generalized
from that sample to all PPO models was particularly vulnerable to model bias.

The corrected diagnostic supports a local Trip-timing benefit on both stages,
and Trip before Cleave on M5. It does not establish a consistent average benefit
for the other scored disagreement types. The prior whole-fight intervention
results remain relevant and unchanged; frequency times local values should not
be presented as a causal allocation of PPO's gap.

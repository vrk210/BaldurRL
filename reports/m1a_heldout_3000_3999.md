# M1A held-out evaluation: seeds 3000–3999

M1A randomizes one opponent on every reset. All five policies used the same 1,000 environment seeds. PPO used terminal-only reward, training seeds 0, 1, and 2, 200,000 requested timesteps per seed (200,704 actual), the same hyperparameters, and model-selection seeds 1000–1199. Predictions were deterministic and masked. The simple heuristic retained the M0 rule: heal at HP ≤ 10, otherwise attack, use Action Surge with no Action, otherwise end turn. It did not inspect enemy statistics.

PPO win rates were **95.7%, 96.2%, 95.9%**; mean **95.93%**, sample standard deviation across training seeds **0.25 percentage points**. Training wall times were **29.66, 30.88, 30.14 seconds** for seeds 0–2. Action counts and per-fight use rates are in the local run cards under `runs/m1a_heldout/`.

The best 1,000-episode PPO result exceeded the heuristic by 1.2 percentage points, small relative to evaluation uncertainty. A predeclared fresh, non-overlapping 5,000-seed range (4000–8999) gave Heuristic **4800/5000 (96.00%)** and PPO seeds 0–2 **4790/5000 (95.80%)**, **4801/5000 (96.02%)**, **4792/5000 (95.84%)**. This does not support a meaningful PPO gain. M1A is still close to saturated under this policy set, despite visible randomized enemy statistics.

Decision traces show a policy difference without a reliable outcome gain: PPO seed 1 used Action Surge in 66.6% of fights versus 93.6% for the heuristic, often delaying the extra attack. On seed 3004, after reaching 14 HP with no Action and one Bonus Action, the heuristic ended the turn while PPO seed 1 used Second Wind. The first divergence identifies different decisions; later dice rolls can be consumed in a different order, so it does not by itself prove a causal outcome change.

Detailed traces, run cards, summaries, and models are under ignored `runs/`; no large artifacts are included here.
